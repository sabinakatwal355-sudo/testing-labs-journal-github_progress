# Part 2 – Computer Disassembly
## Before Opening the Computer
Before opening the computer, the system is housed in a standard black metallic and plastic ATX mid-tower chassis. The exterior is rigid and designed to protect the internal components from dust and physical damage. On the front panel, there are accessible features including a power button, status LEDs, a couple of USB ports, and audio jacks for headphones and a microphone. The side panel is secured tightly to the main frame using thumbscrews. Looking at the rear of the case, the back panel exposes the Input/Output (I/O) shield of the motherboard (featuring Ethernet, USB, and audio ports), the video outputs from the GPU, and the ventilation exhaust fan alongside the power supply unit (PSU) main switch and power socket.

## Internal Layout
Open the computer case and observe the internal components.
Describe what you see.
Upon removing the side panel to reveal the internal layout, the largest visible component is the motherboard, which is mounted vertically against the back wall of the chassis. Centralized on the motherboard is a large CPU cooling fan and heatsink assembly, with rows of RAM sticks slotted immediately to its side. A dedicated graphics card (GPU) is seated horizontally into the main PCIe slot, extending across the middle of the case. In the lower quadrant, the Power Supply Unit (PSU) is housed beneath a shroud, with a bundle of thick power cables (like the 24-pin ATX and PCIe cables) routing through the back of the case to distribute power. Storage drives (SSDs/HDDs) are mounted in dedicated bays near the front or flat against the chassis frame, all interconnected via SATA or data cables, leaving open space in the center to allow optimal airflow from the front intake fans to the rear exhaust.
