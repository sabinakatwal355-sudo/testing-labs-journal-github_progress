# Part 5 – Final Reflection
Write **200–300 words** describing your learning experience during this lab.
Consider:
* What surprised you about computer hardware?
* What components were new to you?
* What challenges did you face?
* What did you learn about embedded systems?
* This computer hardware and disassembly lab provided an engaging, hands-on learning experience that bridged the gap between abstract computing concepts and tangible hardware. What surprised me most was the physical layout and structural complexity hidden inside a standard computer case. Seeing how neatly everything fits together—and realizing just how massive and critical the cooling assemblies and power supply units are compared to the main processing chips—was eye-opening.

While traditional PC parts like standard storage drives and RAM modules were familiar by name, working directly with a single-board computer like the Raspberry Pi was entirely new to me. Identifying its microscale integrated components, such as the System on Chip (SoC) and the open layout of the GPIO pins, gave me a much clearer perspective on alternative hardware architectures.

The primary challenge I faced during the lab was the initial learning curve of utilizing Git and GitHub Classroom for managing documentation. Navigating markdown formatting, managing file paths for hardware images, and pushing updates via the online repository required careful attention to detail to ensure the tables and files rendered correctly without syntax errors.

Ultimately, this lab taught me that embedded systems represent a masterful balance of engineering and efficiency. Unlike traditional modular desktops, devices like the Raspberry Pi optimize every millimeter of space by integrating critical computing units onto a single board. I learned that this architecture dramatically reduces manufacturing costs and power consumption, proving that highly capable computing doesn't always require a massive physical footprint.
